## 1. Excellence

### 1.1 Objectives

UMBRAMED-LVC addresses low-value administrative care in European primary healthcare, with an initial focus on chronic prescription renewal workflows. The objective is to reduce time spent by physicians on repetitive but safety-critical administrative decisions without degrading clinical quality or patient safety.

Primary objectives:
- quantify the scale and sources of low-value prescription renewal work in primary care
- validate an AI-assisted workflow that combines clinical reasoning with deterministic safety checks
- demonstrate measurable reduction in physician administrative burden
- generate evidence for scalable deployment across European health systems

### 1.2 Relation to the work programme

The proposal directly targets low-value care by focusing on administrative activity that consumes clinician time without improving patient outcomes when performed manually at high volume. Chronic prescription renewal is a representative case of low-value administrative burden because physicians repeatedly re-evaluate routine renewals using fragmented EHR workflows, despite the fact that many cases follow established protocol logic.

The project reframes this burden as a care-quality and health-system efficiency problem, not merely a software productivity issue. Reducing this category of low-value work can release clinician time for higher-value patient interaction, improve consistency of protocol adherence, and reduce avoidable delays in medication continuation.

### 1.3 Concept and methodology

The proposed methodology combines:
- retrospective workflow analysis on real primary-care renewal cases
- AI-assisted recommendation generation using a clinical LLM
- deterministic safety verification for interactions, contraindications, and dosage constraints
- physician-in-the-loop validation to maintain clinical accountability

The initial clinical context is grounded in the Diraya workflow experience associated with Dr. Valerio Trigos and a 6,500-patient panel. This provides a realistic starting point for modelling administrative friction in prescription renewal tasks.

Methodological phases:
- map the current renewal workflow and identify measurable low-value steps
- build and validate a structured benchmark dataset of renewal cases
- compare manual workflow, alert-only systems, and AI-assisted review
- measure time savings, safety outcomes, error patterns, and clinician acceptance
- develop implementation guidance for broader European transferability

### 1.4 Ambition

Current digital systems largely provide passive alerts. UMBRAMED-LVC aims to move beyond alerts into safe task compression: reducing manual navigation, review, and documentation effort while keeping physicians in control. The ambition is to establish evidence that low-value administrative care can be reduced at scale through clinically governed AI, creating a transferable model for other repetitive healthcare processes.

## 2. Impact

### 2.1 Expected outcomes

Expected outcomes include:
- measurable reduction in physician time spent on prescription renewal administration
- validated evidence of safety-constrained AI support in a real clinical workflow context
- improved consistency of renewal assessment against protocol-based rules
- a reusable evidence base for future deployment in additional European health systems

### 2.2 Pathway to impact

The impact pathway runs from clinical workflow mapping to validated intervention and then to transferability:

1. establish a strong evidence base for the scale of low-value renewal work
2. validate AI-assisted reduction of administrative burden
3. publish implementation and evaluation findings
4. support future procurement, scaling, and regulatory planning

The proposal can generate value at several levels:
- **Clinicians**: more time for direct care and reduced repetitive cognitive load
- **Health systems**: better productivity and more standardised renewal decisions
- **Patients**: reduced delays and fewer administrative bottlenecks around chronic medication continuation
- **European ecosystem**: a concrete model for eliminating low-value digital care tasks without sacrificing safety

### 2.3 Measures to maximise impact

Planned measures:
- scientific dissemination of methodology and results
- publication of implementation guidance and benchmark design
- structured stakeholder engagement with primary-care operators and health-system decision makers
- exploitation planning for future deployment and additional workflow modules
